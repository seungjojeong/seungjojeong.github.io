Display

A vocabulary for how a work was hung · Seungjo Jeong

Schema.org can say that an exhibition featured a work, but not where the work hung, beside what, in what arrangement, at what height or under what light. Those facts belong neither to the work nor to the exhibition alone, but to the pairing of the two. These terms record them, each in a Display record of its own.

They are used in the second block of structured data on the exhibition pages of this site: Woven Memory, Contemporary British Painting Prize 2022, Hopscotch: RCA Research Biennale, “Hello, World!” and the Viva Exhibition.

Terms

display:Display

Class

One work as hung in one exhibition. Its properties describe that hanging: the wall, what hung beside it, how its parts were arranged, how high it hung and how it was lit. A work shown in two exhibitions has two Display records, which can differ without contradicting each other.

A photograph of the hanging can list the Display record among the things it is about, beside the work it shows.

CIDOC CRM: E7 (an activity that forms part of the exhibition).

display:work

Property of a Display · value: VisualArtwork

The work that was hung. Every Display has exactly one.

CIDOC CRM: P16 (used specific object).

display:exhibition

Property of a Display · value: ExhibitionEvent

The exhibition in which it was hung, or one showing of an exhibition that toured, when the hangings differed between venues. Every Display has exactly one.

CIDOC CRM: P9i (forms part of).

display:wall

Property of a Display · value: Place

The wall it hung on, described as a Place within the room.

CIDOC CRM: P7 (took place at).

display:nextTo

Property of a Display · value: VisualArtwork or LocationFeatureSpecification

What hung or stood beside it: another work, or a feature of the room such as a window. It says nothing about direction; the order along a wall is given in the wall's description.

display:arrangement

Property of a Display · value: Text

How its parts were arranged on the wall for this exhibition, in words: the order of the panels, or how a polyptych was laid out.

The order of the panels can also be given as data with panelOrder; arrangement keeps what a list can’t say, such as how far one panel sat below another.

display:lighting

Property of a Display · value: LocationFeatureSpecification

The lighting in the room that fell on it, as one of the room's features.

display:bottomEdgeAboveFloor

Property of a Display · value: QuantitativeValue

The height of its lowest edge above the floor, in centimetres (unitCode CMT).

If the height was estimated rather than measured, give the range as minValue and maxValue, say how it was found in the value’s description, and name the photograph it was read from with estimatedFrom.

display:topEdgeBelowCeiling

Property of a Display · value: QuantitativeValue

The distance from its top edge to the ceiling, in centimetres (unitCode CMT). Use maxValue when only an upper limit is known.

display:estimatedFrom

Property of a QuantitativeValue · value: ImageObject

The photograph from which an estimated value was worked out, such as a height read off an installation view. It goes inside the value it qualifies, so that the estimate points to its evidence and can be checked against it.

Schema.org has no way to say where a value came from. CIDOC CRM: P16 (used specific object), with the estimate read as a measurement (E16) that used the photograph.

display:panelOrder

Property of a Display · value: a list of VisualArtwork

The work’s panels in the order they hung, from left to right as seen facing the wall; where one panel hung above another, the upper one comes first. Each item is one of the work’s own panel records (the parts it lists under hasPart), so the same panel can be followed from one hanging to the next, and two hangings of the work can be compared by a machine.

Written as a JSON-LD list (@list), because the order is the point. The panel records’ numbers are labels, not an order: tell the panels apart by their names or descriptions, such as the colour of each panel.

Example

The hanging of Bodily Memories at the Viva Exhibition. Its record on the “Hello, World!” page gives a different panel order, for a different hanging of the same painting: there the list runs panel 1, 2, 3 (white, gold, black). The height was read off installation view 04, whose own record lists this Display among the things it is about.

Related vocabularies

In CIDOC CRM terms, a Display can be read as an activity (E7) that forms part of the exhibition (P9i), used a specific object (P16) and took place at the wall (P7). The notes under each term give the nearest CRM class or property; they are pointers, not claims of equivalence.
